Wonder and Method business plan V5

Use V5 on its own. It contains the current business plan, working instructions, research and complete historical snapshots of V1–V4. Earlier decisions that changed are identified in the carry-forward record; historical prices never override the current offer.

The business is simple: build a clearly scoped, owned website for a service business, then provide optional care or useful search improvements. Start with Erie County, New York and serve other buyers online. Show distinctive work, clear terms and verifiable delivery rather than claiming to be the cheapest or best.

The four things to use

  1. Offers: two website choices and optional services, with the exact purchase boundaries.
  2. Run the business: one client flow, one weekly board, one accepted scope and one work receipt.
  3. Build brief: the website requirements and release checks, including restored technical safeguards.
  4. Reference: finance, brand, keywords, templates, evidence and the earlier versions when needed.

The main prices remain $6,000 Focused / $9,500 Signature. Optional services are $199/month Care, $1,500 Search Sprint, and $1,100/month Search. A documented new-build baseline avoids a forced Sprint. No subscription is required to keep a paid website.

What is simpler

There is no mandatory 85-hour practice build or week-seven booking delay. Demonstrate the relevant capabilities using actual or existing work, then book only what fits the confirmed calendar. Full client scope still passes acceptance before launch.

Use the tools already available. A portal, elaborate agent system, seven-part prompt library and separate trackers are not launch requirements. The founder controls facts, integration and release; parallel tasks are useful when their review fits the calendar.

What comes next

Confirm actual weekly availability, cash floor, identity/contact and account ownership. Build the studio from the retained brand, search and route plan. Record the relevant form, mobile, deployment and handoff evidence as work progresses. Then run bounded paid projects and measure the result.

The unresolved business inputs are visible in the runbook; they do not prevent preparing the site. No paid-client results or measured production-speed advantage are claimed. Decision

Use the Wonder / Method Ink palette: a deep blue-black ink, warm paper, one clay accent, and two dark chromatic link accents. The combination gives the name room for a little surprise while keeping reading, hierarchy, scope, and proof exact. The visual system should feel edited rather than ornamental: one expressive move per section, generous space, crisp rules, and copy that names its limits.

Do not present blue as inherently trustworthy or any color as a guaranteed conversion device. Elliot and Maier’s review says color effects have boundary conditions and limited real-world generalizability (Annual Review of Psychology, 2014, checked 2026-10-13). Palmer and Schloss’ ecological-valence account links preference to responses to color-associated objects rather than a universal color dictionary (PNAS, 2010, checked 2026-10-13). Labrecque and Milne found hue, saturation, and value can map to brand-personality dimensions in their studies, but that is evidence for a testable direction, not a promise about this audience (JAMS, 2012, checked 2026-10-13).

Evidence translated into design rules

Perceived quality is helped by a coherent, easy-to-parse composition. Reber, Schwarz, and Winkielman’s processing-fluency review connects ease of processing with aesthetic pleasure, while acknowledging that repetition, symmetry, figure-ground contrast, and prototypicality operate through a broader perceptual process (PSPR, 2004, checked 2026-10-13). In a website study, Tuch and colleagues found that visual complexity and prototypicality influenced first-impression ratings in exposures as short as 17–50 ms; lower complexity and higher prototypicality were rated more appealing in that study (IJHCS, 2012, checked 2026-10-13). This supports a restrained shell around one memorable device; it does not establish that a particular layout will sell.

Hall and Hanna found that higher text/background contrast generally improved readability in their web-page experiment, while aesthetic ratings related to purchase intention in the tested pages (Behaviour & Information Technology, 2004, checked 2026-10-13). Treat this as a reason to protect contrast and legibility, not as a conversion statistic. Ling and van Schaik found little task-performance effect from font choice in their experiments, while line length affected scanning and subjective outcomes (IJHCS, 2006, checked 2026-10-13). Therefore choose type by role, coverage, rendering, and licensing; constrain prose width rather than claiming that one typeface is universally best.

WCAG 2.2 sets a 4.5:1 minimum contrast ratio for normal text and 3:1 for large text at Level AA (W3C 1.4.3, checked 2026-10-13). Meaningful controls, states, and graphics need 3:1 against adjacent colors (W3C 1.4.11, checked 2026-10-13). Color cannot be the only cue for meaning (W3C 1.4.1, checked 2026-10-13). These are the acceptance thresholds for this system; the tokens file records actual pairs and calculated ratios.

WCAG 2.2’s mobile-relevant reflow criterion requires vertical content to work at the equivalent of 320 CSS px without losing information or functionality or requiring two-dimensional scrolling, with explicit exceptions for content whose meaning needs two dimensions (W3C mobile guidance, checked 2026-10-13). A 24 × 24 CSS px target is the AA minimum with spacing/equivalent exceptions (W3C 2.5.8, checked 2026-10-13). This product should target 48 × 48 CSS px hit areas for primary controls because web.dev and Material guidance recommend that size for touch usability (web.dev form design, Material touch targets, checked 2026-10-13). The 48 px target is a product quality target, not a claim that WCAG requires 48 px.
