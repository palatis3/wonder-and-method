# Run the business

Use one client flow, three working records and the tools already available. The current offer is in offers.json; this runbook controls delivery. Research notes and historical plans do not change a sold promise.

## One flow

| Step | Do the work | Move on when |
|---|---|---|
| Fit | Understand the buyer job, editing needs, facts/access, approver, timing and investment | A current offer fits, or give a clear separate-scope/referral/no-fit answer |
| Quote and book | Agree one scope/cost sheet; check relevant capability evidence, actual dates and cash obligations | Written scope, named approver, ready inputs and booking payment meet agreed conditions |
| Design and build | Work from approved facts and direction; use bounded parallel tasks; one design round | Founder has integrated and reviewed the staging version |
| Approve and launch | Run real QA; one staging round; collect written acceptance and agreed payment | Critical checks pass, exact version/target approved, recovery and staffed launch window ready |
| Correct and hand over | Honor 30-day defects; give paid source/accounts/licenses and operating notes | Defects and remaining obligations have owners/dates; client can use the paid work |
| Optional service | Do Care checks or the agreed Search task; issue one receipt | Next task/renewal is useful and approved, otherwise pause or complete handoff |

Changes, incidents and client delays are entries on the same job, not new management systems.

## Three records

**Weekly board.** One row per lead or client, with current phase, next action/date, accountable owner, blocker, accepted scope link, booked/actual founder hours, milestone/launch/correction dates and any renewal/exit. A researched business is marked research, not a qualified opportunity. Store volunteered business contact details only where needed to respond or deliver; do not create private profiles. No response, compliment or test inquiry counts as a win.

**Accepted scope.** One versioned buyer-job, deliverable, cost, ownership and date sheet. It includes editing/CMS choice, facts/rights, approver, acceptance tests, external bills and service terms. The signed terms determine earned work and cancellation reconciliation. The project fact sheet lives with this scope.

**Work receipt.** One cumulative evidence record. Append completed work, decisions, actual tests, changes, incidents, releases, time and monthly service notes. Link artifacts rather than copying them into several trackers. Use the existing invoice system for money and secure access manager for credentials; never duplicate secrets in the board or receipt.

The [template pack](SERVICE_PLAYBOOK.md) supplies short blocks for these records. There is no required portal, CRM migration, custom automation platform or seven-prompt library.

## Book from capability and a real calendar

No mandatory 85-hour practice build or week-seven start rule. Use existing dated equivalent work or test the missing capability on the actual studio/starter:

- A real responsive page demonstrates visual quality, narrow-width behavior, keyboard, zoom/reflow and reduced motion.
- A six-field synthetic inquiry reaches both the provider record and intended inbox; success/error behavior works. Supplied privacy markup and a simple redirect mechanism are exercised.
- Client-controlled source, reproducible deploy, rollback and the handoff path work; secrets stay out of source and prompts.
- Signature additionally needs evidence that its agreed hero treatment, responsive/poster/reduced-motion fallback and rights/handoff can be delivered. Focused need not wait for Signature media.
- The accepted scope and actual calendar include design/review/QA/launch, client input deadlines and the correction tail, plus a cash check for unfinished obligations.

These tests demonstrate capability, not paid-client outcomes or proven 55/85-hour profitability. The full applicable scope—pages, word limits, actual form, supplied privacy, up to ten real supplied redirects and agreed hero—must pass each client's launch acceptance. Validate maximum-scope timing in studio work or controlled paid pilots before increasing throughput claims. Do not manufacture unnecessary public pages or count unneeded sample writing as a launch prerequisite.

## One capacity rule

For each actual week: available founder hours minus dated studio, sales and admin work equals the potential client capacity. Initially leave 20% of that capacity uncommitted for unexpected work. Book the remainder only when stage peaks, approvals, launches, recurring dates and the following correction period fit. Holidays, leave and outside obligations reduce available hours first.

The 13-week scenarios retain 145 studio, 78 sales and 52 admin hours, but are illustrations—not availability or sales forecasts. The studio allocation is 107h build/validation, 24h for three evidence-ready launch guides and 14h for two later guides. Shorten or rephase it only from actual progress and evidence; don't spend all estimated hours as a quota.

The conservative example schedules at most 20 planned client hours/week; outer review guards remain 24 planned/week, 96 planned/calendar month and 120 total client/calendar month. These caps do not override a smaller real calendar. The dated examples and month checks are in founder-calendar.json. A project waiting for client input can coexist with another, but resumed work must have a real slot. Separate launch peaks; don't assume two open projects mean two simultaneous full workloads.

Count hours once. Focused's 55 and Signature's 85 already include known revisions, handoff and expected correction work; place that correction portion on the actual 30-day tail, even beyond the example horizon. Search's seven hours include its one-hour internal reserve. Care's 1.25-hour average includes routine coordination and allocated quarterly work. The shared unexpected-work pool is additional protection for overruns/incidents, not extra sellable capacity. Add known activation/exit labor once when it is not already in the accepted job; acquisition time belongs in sales allocation. Unattended renders are elapsed time; human review, fixes and integration are founder labor.

## Keep the work moving

One named approver returns one consolidated list. Missing facts, rights or decisions produce a written blocker and revised available slot; never invent facts or infer approval from silence. A priced change states deliverable, acceptance, fee and date effect before approval. Do not make the client chase an unexplained missed milestone.

Use one reusable AI task pattern: accepted scope/version, owned paths, allowed facts, desired result, tests and stop conditions. Parallelize independent file areas or renders, then let the founder integrate, check factual claims and approve release. Store prompt variants only if repeated use saves net time after review and corrections. No separate orchestration layer is required.

A critical failure blocks release: broken inquiry path, missing actual inbox receipt, exposed secret, unapproved claim, unlicensed asset, indexable confidential staging, critical accessibility barrier, missing rollback or missing exact-version acceptance. The build brief contains the detailed checklist.

## Monthly service and exceptions

Care requires actual staffed hours, closures, recipient and escalation path before enrollment. Its included incident action is verify, notify and route—not unlimited repair. Automated monitoring is not 24/7 staffing. Test actual mailbox receipt monthly; run the quarterly dependency/recovery review and record it. One calm month or unused edit allowance does not erase the check work bought by the client.

Five business days before Search renewal, approve one useful task and required inputs or pause the next invoice. If charged without a task, the client chooses refund or credit for the unused cycle. Work begun is itemized and the remainder reconciled. A blocker never quietly turns a fixed implementation purchase into an audit-only report. Keep Care's requested edit distinct from Search's page/fix; common release QA need only run once.

For an incident, record impact, first seen, owner, last good release, provider/form/DNS checks, current action, client notice and next update. Use the tested rollback or agreed workaround when appropriate. For an absence, state actual closures/coverage before commitments; do not invent backup staff. Keep client accounts and recovery instructions usable. If a sold promise cannot be staffed, arrange an agreed service adjustment or qualified cover before the absence.

On exit, stop future renewal, preserve client-owned access and paid work, reconcile the dated work/payment ledger, provide existing paid source/assets/licenses/build notes within five business days and record remaining items. Remove delegated access after verification. Record data export, retention/deletion owner and action. Only new migration labor is separately quoted.

## Weekly review and learning

Review the next four weeks plus correction tails; cash collected versus earned obligations; overdue input/approval; actual delivery/sales hours; recurring tasks/renewals; issues; and the next useful acquisition action. Update the one board. Change or defer low-value internal work before consuming recovery as normal overtime.

Measure three completed paid builds per tier before expanding throughput claims. Two comparable jobs more than 20% over estimate trigger a pause in new timing promises and a stage-level diagnosis. Honor existing commitments. Care scaling needs a full paid quarter including quarterly work, incidents and lifecycle costs; Search needs two complete paid cycles. These are learning checkpoints, not bans on a second well-scoped client when dated capacity permits it.

Keep proposals and win/loss reasons alongside hours and receipts. Unknown attribution stays unknown. A beautiful owned demo proves craft, not paid demand. Actual calendar, cash floor, required compensation, selected provider bills and coverage are the few unresolved inputs to fill before the commitments that rely on them.
