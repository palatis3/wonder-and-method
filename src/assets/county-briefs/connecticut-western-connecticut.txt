EXAMPLE (MADE UP)
Fictional example: A Stamford adviser receives a new request while two agreed tasks are queued. The customer names the priority, and the adviser records which earlier task moves back and whether started work must pause. The updated queue makes the tradeoff visible instead of implying every request becomes urgent at once.
Build a fictional queue with competing requests and one task already started. Insert a new priority and document the displaced work and approval. Then apply the approved unused-capacity rule to a quiet period, recording what carries forward or ends and who confirms it.
