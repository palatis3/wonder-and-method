COMPLETED REDESIGN BRIEF — FICTIONAL EXAMPLE
Updated October 21, 2026
Source: https://wonderandmethod.com/assets/audit-implementation/illustrative-completed-brief.txt

The business, people, website and scenario below are made up. This is the same six-section form as the blank redesign brief, not a client engagement or result.

1. BUSINESS AND CUSTOMER
Business name: Example Home Repair (fictional).
Primary service(s): Planned small interior repairs.
Ideal customer: Homeowners with non-emergency repair jobs.
Primary customer action on the website: Request an estimate.
Service geography and online/in-person delivery model: Example coverage in Erie County, New York; work at the customer’s property. Final coverage must be confirmed by a real business.
Jobs you accept and jobs you do not: Accept agreed interior repairs; exclude emergencies and structural work in this fictional example.

2. EXISTING SITE AND CONTENT
Current website: example.invalid (not a live website).
What works today: The service explanation and business-owned accounts.
What causes confusion: The Contact button is hard to find on a phone.
Current URLs and proposed pages: Home, Services and Contact. Keep their existing addresses where appropriate.
Pages that receive useful inquiries or search visits: Unknown in this example; ask the owner for available records.
Approved facts, photographs and supporting evidence: Owner-supplied service facts and real project photos, with permission confirmed before publication.
Content and media-rights approver: Alex, the fictional business owner.
Redirects needed (use the separate URL-map worksheet): List useful old addresses in the URL-map worksheet; confirm whether any actually move.

3. EDITING AND FEATURES
Who edits after handoff: The developer or optional care service makes code changes.
How often and what changes: Opening hours and photographs a few times a year.
Can a developer make changes, or do you need a log-in editor: Developer changes are suitable; no log-in page editor is included.
Required form fields and who receives inquiries: Name, email, town, job type and short description; one business inbox receives requests.
Required booking, payment, account or integration tools: One ready booking link, if the client-owned account is suitable; no online payment.
Selected package inclusions and their agreed limits: Launch: up to six marketing pages, one simple form and one ready connection; this example uses three pages.
Additions beyond that included scope, if any: Outside booking-account setup and anything beyond the agreed package are separately quoted.
Provider accounts, owners, costs and renewal dates: Domain, hosting and any booking or email provider; actual prices and renewals still need confirmation.

4. PROJECT DECISIONS
One named approver: Alex (fictional owner).
Budget and excluded/unknown provider costs: Launch studio fee as listed on the current pricing page; taxes and actual provider bills to be confirmed.
Input-ready dates: Agree dates once facts, photos and account invitations are ready.
Realistic review windows: One design review and one finished-site review, each up to five business days on agreed dates.
Required page, mobile, keyboard, form and recovery checks: Agreed pages, phone layout, keyboard use, form send/receive/reply test and a safe recovery plan.
Launch version and destination to approve: The exact approved preview version and client-owned public domain, both to be recorded.
Should Google be allowed to list the website at launch: Yes after written launch approval; keep the unfinished preview excluded.

5. HANDOFF
Website files you paid for and image licenses: Paid website source, approved text and images, licenses and publishing instructions.
Account access and provider inventory: Business retains account control; use suitable invitations and a separate secure method for secrets.
Inquiry export, retention and recovery process: Confirm how the actual form system stores, exports and removes inquiries; do not assume one design fits every platform.
Defect-correction period and optional later services: 30 calendar days after launch for in-scope build faults; later care is optional.
Open questions: Confirm provider costs, booking-account readiness and any useful old links.

6. ASK EACH DESIGNER TO RETURN
Included pages, writing and features: State the three-page plan, writing, form and ready booking-link scope in the proposal.
What is excluded or needs a separate quote: List new account setup, extra pages and any unsupported tool work.
Assumptions and information needed from you: Approved facts, permissioned photos, suitable access and one approver.
One-time price and payment stages: Current Launch price; 50% booking, 30% written design approval, 20% written finished-site approval before launch.
Outside provider bills and optional ongoing help: Itemize actual provider bills and any separately chosen monthly help.
Schedule, start conditions and review dates: Confirm the build target and both owner-review windows on calendar dates.
How changes and feedback are handled: One combined feedback list per included round; new scope priced in writing.
Who controls the accounts, files and licenses: The business controls its accounts and receives paid custom files subject to third-party licenses.
Launch checks, recovery and handoff: Record launch checks and recovery responsibilities; deliver existing paid files within five business days of completion or settled cancellation.
Open questions: Resolve any unanswered scope, cost or access question before booking.
