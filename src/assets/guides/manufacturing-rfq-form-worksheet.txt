Give a manufacturing quote request enough context to review
Wonder and Method — planning worksheet
Source article: https://wonderandmethod.com/guides/manufacturing-rfq-form/

Use your actual business details. This worksheet is not an operator reservation, approval or quote.

Request component | What the customer supplies | What your team confirms
Project stage | Drawing, concept or existing part | Appropriate review route
Part identity | Part name and revision | One current version
Quantity | Initial quantity and repeat need | Quote scope
Requirements | Material, finish and critical features | Missing or conflicting specifications
Evidence package | File list or sample description | Receipt and readable contents
Production dimensions | Source of measurements | Required verification owner

Review case
Try a buyer who uploads revision B, then emails revision C without mentioning the change. One attachment exceeds the form limit, and the quantity is described only as a trial batch. The request should remain reviewable without treating either drawing as approved. Check that staff can identify the current version, ask for a quantity and tell the buyer exactly which file is missing.

Facts to confirm
Bring an anonymized inquiry that your estimator could not quote, the current accepted-file policy and a list of decisions that require technical review. Use these to select fields and response states. The worksheet describes information flow; it does not decide tolerances, manufacturability or the security controls appropriate to a particular project.
