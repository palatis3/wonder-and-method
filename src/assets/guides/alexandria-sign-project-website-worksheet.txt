Stage | Record to keep | Meaning of completion
Site inquiry | Address, position and supplied photos | Site context received
Scope agreement | Design, fabrication, review and install owners | Responsibilities agreed
Customer proof | Approved artwork version | Customer approves that design
Historic review | Route and status confirmed with the city | Relevant decision recorded
Permit process | Applicable authority reference and status | Required record reviewed by the owner
Installation review | Outstanding conditions and agreed scope | Responsible staff confirm readiness

Review case
Test a customer who approves an unlit wall-sign proof, then asks for lighting and a different position while expecting the original installation date. Another company is handling permitting, but no one has recorded its latest status. The website should preserve the approved version, surface the requested change and route it for review. It should not carry the old ready-to-install message into the changed project.

Facts to confirm
Bring your quotation scope, proof-approval wording and a real project timeline with identifying details removed. Mark where responsibility changes between customer, designer, permit provider and installer. Link current city guidance beside the relevant stage. This board does not establish permitted dimensions, an approval route, review timing or legal compliance; those decisions require the actual project's responsible reviewers.
