# Make a custom print inquiry distinct from an accepted production order

Researched website-planning draft · October 22, 2026 · not published

**For:** Custom printers, makers and studios whose orders need specification and feasibility review.

An attractive contact form can create the wrong expectation if customers believe sending it reserves production time. A custom-order website should first establish whether the job is feasible, then identify the actual submission route and the evidence that the order has been accepted.

## What the sources establish

- **Davidson County, TN:** Hatch lists typical turnaround of ten weeks or longer. Custom orders are not accepted electronically or by telephone; customers submit an order form by fax, mail or drop-off. Price depends on color count, and supplied logos require suitable artwork and a photoplate fee. [Hatch Show Print Custom Prints and Design](https://hatchshowprint.com/custom-prints-design).

These are particular operators’ published policies as reviewed on the research date, not county-wide practices. The recommendations below are original analysis and proposed interface specifications. They do not describe a completed project, measured result, partnership, field visit or service offered by Wonder and Method.

## Qualify the deadline before the artwork

Hatch Show Print publishes a typical turnaround of ten weeks or longer. That is not a guaranteed completion date, but it is useful early context for a rush customer. Present the timing expectation near the inquiry and ask for the required in-hand date before encouraging extensive specification work.

## Collect the details that change the quote

Quantity, color count and artwork preparation affect the cited process. A useful intake explains which inputs are ready, which need discussion, and whether a supplied logo requires production preparation. Avoid quoting a universal price from a short marketing description.

## Respect the real ordering channel

The reviewed Hatch page directs custom-order submission through its order form by fax, mail or drop-off, rather than electronic or telephone orders. An article about website planning must not invent an online checkout on Hatch’s behalf. Another studio should expose its own accepted channel just as clearly.

## Proposed artifact

Illustrative specification for adaptation to a real business. Bracketed text requires the responsible owner’s actual details.

| Planning step | Proposed field or message | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Required in-hand date and intended event date, if different | Feasibility request only. |
| Specification | Quantity, colors and intended print format | Inputs for review, not a final price. |
| Artwork | Available artwork and preparation questions | Confirm the studio’s actual file requirements. |
| Submission | Use [the studio’s accepted order route] | Sending a website inquiry is not automatically an order. |
| Acceptance | We will confirm specification, price and schedule through [actual process] | Production is accepted only when that process completes. |

## Review case

A customer needs prints next month and submits an inquiry. The confirmation must not say their production slot is booked. A customer sends a logo without suitable production artwork; the process should flag the preparation decision before final pricing.

**Evidence still needed before using this for a business:** The studio’s real lead times, capacity, accepted ordering route, artwork requirements, deposits and approval process. No claim that this research changed Hatch’s website or improved its sales.

**Why the source example matters:** The source establishes a concrete mismatch between a generic digital contact form and the real production-order process.

**Editorial placement:** A useful maker-order guide based on a Davidson County example; combine only with examples that add a different order decision.
