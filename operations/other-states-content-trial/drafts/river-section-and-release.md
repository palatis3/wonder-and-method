# Match the river section and release date to the trip being sold

Researched website-planning draft · October 22, 2026 · not published

**For:** Rafting operators and accommodation businesses referring customers to release-dependent river trips.

A river name is not a complete product specification. Sections can differ in difficulty, operating dates, inclusions and suitability for a party. A useful selection page identifies the actual section before it presents a departure or combines activities into an itinerary.

## What the sources establish

- **Polk County, TN:** OAR describes Upper, Middle and Lower river sections as separately dam-controlled. Its Full River trip combines Upper and Middle with lunch; the Lower offers calmer tubing and group-raft alternatives. [OAR Ocoee River Release Schedule](https://ocoee.com/ocoee-release-schedule/).
- **Fayette County, WV:** NPS describes a six-weekend autumn Gauley season, publishes the 2026 planned release dates and distinguishes Upper and Lower sections with different difficulty and participation expectations. [Gauley River Whitewater | NPS](https://www.nps.gov/gari/planyourvisit/whitewater.htm).

These are particular operators’ published policies as reviewed on the research date, not county-wide practices. The recommendations below are original analysis and proposed interface specifications. They do not describe a completed project, measured result, partnership, field visit or service offered by Wonder and Method.

## Keep each section’s product distinct

OAR distinguishes separately controlled Ocoee sections and describes its Full River product as Upper plus Middle with lunch. Its Lower-river alternatives are different activities, not simply a quieter version of the same departure. The page should not swap one for another without explaining the change.

## Use the calendar as a dependency

NPS describes a planned seasonal Gauley release pattern and different sections. Planned releases are not a guarantee of flows, operating conditions or the seller’s available places. Keep the current calendar linked to the selected section and let the operator confirm the actual trip.

## Let the operator define participation requirements

The page can ask for party details needed to select an appropriate product, but the business must supply and approve its actual eligibility rules. Do not borrow another outfitter’s age limits or turn general regional information into individualized safety advice.

## Proposed artifact

Illustrative specification for adaptation to a real business. Bracketed text requires the responsible owner’s actual details.

| Trip chooser | Proposed behavior | Confirmation needed |
|---|---|---|
| Section and product | Named section, or a clearly defined combined trip | Operator’s actual route and inclusions. |
| Preferred date | Check the current section-specific release calendar | Availability and operating decision remain with operator. |
| Party | Collect only details the operator needs for its participation rules | Owner approves eligibility wording. |
| Alternative activity | Describe tubing or another offered option as a different product | Do not silently substitute it for whitewater. |
| Status | Requested / confirmed departure / operating update | Keep planned calendar and confirmed service separate. |

## Review case

A date supports one section but not the combined product the customer selected. The website must require a new choice or a conversation. A family considering a lower-river alternative should see its different activity and inclusions before agreeing.

**Evidence still needed before using this for a business:** Current releases, actual trip inventory, meeting points, participation requirements and disruption communication from the responsible operator. Regional river access can cross county boundaries; it does not prove a business is based in Fayette County.

**Why the source example matters:** The examples change product composition and the date-to-product relationship. They belong together rather than in parallel Ocoee and Gauley county essays.

**Editorial placement:** Treat as one operator-planning guide. County attribution is regional context, not a claim of exclusive service territory.
