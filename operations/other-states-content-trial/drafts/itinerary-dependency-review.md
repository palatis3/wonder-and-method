# Check the third-party service before keeping an itinerary on sale

Researched website-planning draft · October 22, 2026 · not published

**For:** Outfitters, lodging businesses and tour sellers whose advertised experiences depend on another operator’s access or transport.

An itinerary can become inaccurate even when the seller’s own service has not changed. A train policy, dock closure or altered return service can break the arrangement. The page needs a way to identify that dependency and withdraw an unsupported promise while the owner verifies it.

## What the sources establish

- **Allegany County, MD:** The current FAQ says bikes are not allowed on the train at this time. It also describes extending a Frostburg stay only when another trip runs the following day. [Western Maryland Scenic Railroad FAQ](https://wmsr.com/faq).
- **Leelanau County, MI:** NPS lists Manitou island docks and other facilities closed until further notice during dock relocation, no potable island water, and limited boat-access arrangements. [Sleeping Bear Dunes Backcountry Camping](https://www.nps.gov/slbe/planyourvisit/backcountrycamping.htm).

These are particular operators’ published policies as reviewed on the research date, not county-wide practices. The recommendations below are original analysis and proposed interface specifications. They do not describe a completed project, measured result, partnership, field visit or service offered by Wonder and Method.

## Identify the claim that relies on someone else

The Western Maryland Scenic Railroad FAQ says bikes are not currently allowed, while older reviews describe bike carriage. A trail package should rely on confirmed current policy rather than a historical customer story. An overnight extension also needs an actual next-day return service.

## Treat conflicting status as a reason to investigate

The reviewed NPS Manitou page combines closure information with stale forward-looking wording. This is evidence of a verification problem, not reliable proof of an indefinite closure. Do not reproduce that state as a permanent destination fact or automatically switch bookings based only on the page.

## Keep a small dependency register

For each affected offer, record the external requirement, authoritative source, person who checks it and the action when it cannot be confirmed. The useful output is a changed customer promise: pause the dependent option, explain what remains available if known, or handle a request manually. A date stamp without that decision is not enough.

## Proposed artifact

Illustrative specification for adaptation to a real business. Bracketed text requires the responsible owner’s actual details.

| Dependency record | Illustrative entry | Action before publishing |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | Train-assisted cycling itinerary | Confirm bike carriage directly with the operator. |
| Return | Overnight extension from Frostburg | Confirm a suitable next-day service exists. |
| Access | Manitou island trip requiring dock or boat access | Resolve current status with NPS and the transport provider. |
| Evidence conflict | Current policy differs from older review or stale page text | Hold the disputed promise pending verification. |
| Customer status | This arrangement is awaiting confirmation; contact us before relying on it | Use only if the business can actually handle that inquiry. |

## Review case

An old review says bicycles were carried. The owner must not approve a train-and-bike offer solely from that review when the current FAQ says otherwise. A dock notice with stale dates must trigger direct confirmation, not a permanent “closed” article.

**Evidence still needed before using this for a business:** Current direct operator confirmation for any sellable dependent itinerary, a named maintenance owner and a real response plan for affected customers. These drafts do not establish live inventory or access.

**Why the source example matters:** The original analysis concerns conflict resolution and retiring unsupported promises, not another destination description.

**Editorial placement:** Combine these two cases with future changed-service examples. Keep the Leelanau example explicitly verification-pending rather than a newly confirmed access guide.
