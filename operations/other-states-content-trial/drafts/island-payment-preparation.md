# Explain payment and parking before guests leave the mainland

Researched website-planning draft · October 22, 2026 · not published

**For:** Island accommodation, tour and activity businesses whose guests must prepare before a crossing.

A payment instruction is useful only while the customer can still act on it. For an island visit, the place to explain cash needs or mainland parking may be the booking confirmation, before the traveler boards, rather than a sign at the business.

## What the sources establish

- **Somerset County, MD:** Smith Island tourism says the nearest bank or ATM is in Crisfield, although some island businesses accept cards or payment apps. Its mainland parking instructions differ between the marina office and the Old Lumber Yard cash pay box. [Smith Island Practical Information](https://visitsmithisland.com/practical-information/).

These are particular operators’ published policies as reviewed on the research date, not county-wide practices. The recommendations below are original analysis and proposed interface specifications. They do not describe a completed project, measured result, partnership, field visit or service offered by Wonder and Method.

## Describe the actual merchant’s payment methods

Smith Island’s visitor information places the nearest bank or ATM in Crisfield while noting that some island businesses accept cards or payment apps. That supports preparation advice, not a statement that the island is cash-only. Each business should list the methods it really accepts and any remaining payment.

## Tie parking instructions to the chosen option

The reviewed mainland parking options have different payment arrangements. Identify the relevant lot and authoritative current instructions rather than give a single cash amount for every arrival. A general island travel page does not prove which lot is best for a specific guest’s operator or trip.

## Separate preparation from collection

The confirmation can explain what the guest may need to bring without collecting bank details or asking them to pay another merchant through the accommodation’s form. Keep each payment owner and timing visible. Refresh the note when the business changes its payment methods.

## Proposed artifact

Illustrative specification for adaptation to a real business. Bracketed text requires the responsible owner’s actual details.

| Pre-departure card | Proposed message | Owner supplies |
|---|---|---|
| Our payment methods | We accept [actual methods] for [remaining amount or service] | Current merchant policy. |
| Mainland preparation | Review cash needs before leaving Crisfield; the island visitor source identifies the nearest ATM there | Only relevant preparation, not a cash-only claim. |
| Parking choice | Use [selected lot] and its current payment instructions | Correct lot and source link. |
| Separate charges | Your crossing, parking and our service are paid to [respective providers] | Accurate inclusions and responsibility. |
| Uncertain plan | Contact [the responsible business] before travel | A monitored support route. |

## Review case

A guest has prepaid lodging but still needs to arrange mainland parking. The confirmation must distinguish what has been paid from what remains. A card-accepting island merchant must not inherit a generic cash-only warning.

**Evidence still needed before using this for a business:** The real business’s payment methods, amounts still due, intended departure operator and appropriate parking option. Recheck the visitor source before launch; no current prices are reproduced here.

**Why the source example matters:** The constraint changes when customer information must appear: before departure while mainland facilities remain available.

**Editorial placement:** Use as a short operational-payment guide beside island arrival planning, not a Somerset County commercial landing page.
