# Name the trolley before promising hotel transportation

Niagara County, New York · researched planning draft · October 22, 2026

**For:** Niagara County lodging businesses explaining how guests reach attractions without using a car.

“The trolley is nearby” can mean several different services around Niagara Falls. A guest deciding whether to bring a car needs to know which service the property means, where to board it and whether it runs during the stay. A hotel’s arrival page can answer that without becoming a copy of three transportation websites.

**Local evidence:** Destination Niagara USA distinguishes the Discover Niagara Shuttle, the Niagara Falls Scenic Trolley within the state park, and NFTA’s Niagara Falls Trolley. Its guide describes seasonal operation and different coverage and fare arrangements. [Source 1](https://www.niagarafallsusa.com/planning-tools/transportation/trolleys/).

The recommendations below are original website-planning analysis. They do not describe a completed client project, measured result or services offered by a particular local operator.

## Connect the property to the correct service

Start with the property’s actual address and verified boarding point. Name the operator and service in the link text. Distinguish transportation between destinations from a trolley operating inside an attraction. If a hotel advertises a guest pass, verify participation and explain where the guest obtains it; the availability of a public service does not establish that the property provides a pass.

## Show the seasonal dependency

Put the operator’s current timetable link beside the transport claim, not only in a general resources footer. If the lodging booking dates fall outside verified service dates, avoid a blanket year-round “car-free stay” promise. A simpler site can state the operating season and require a current schedule check without attempting to calculate itineraries. Give guests the property’s real alternative arrival information.

## Keep directions consistent after booking

The confirmation should use the same service name and boarding location as the arrival page. Staff should be able to update this information in one maintained place. Do not duplicate timetable times across room descriptions, offer pages and automated emails unless the business has a reliable update process. Include a written description of the boarding point so that an embedded map is not the only usable instruction.

## Proposed transport reference panel

A content layout based on the tourism agency’s distinctions. The hotel must verify its own connection and use current operator information before adapting it.

| Service | Sourced distinction | Property-specific field to complete |
|---|---|---|
| Discover Niagara Shuttle | County attractions shuttle; described as seasonal and free in the tourism guide. | Verified boarding stop, current operator timetable link and walking directions from the property. |
| Niagara Falls Scenic Trolley | Operates within Niagara Falls State Park. | Explain how the guest reaches the park; do not use it as the hotel-arrival option unless independently established. |
| NFTA Niagara Falls Trolley | A separate service; the guide describes passes for participating hotel guests. | Confirm whether this property participates and how guests obtain any included pass. |
| Maintenance record | Owner-maintained information, not a timetable copied from the guide. | Checked by; checked date; applicable season; next review date; fallback arrival link. |

Proposed panel heading: “Which service helps you get here?” Show only verified services relevant to the property. Keep the other distinctions in a short explanation if they prevent confusion. The guide’s general fare information should not become an unverified hotel benefit.

## Planning worksheet

| Decision | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Named service | Operator and route name; avoid the generic label “trolley.” |
| Property connection | Verified stop and written directions from the lodging. |
| Season and ticket | Current operator link and any verified hotel-pass arrangement. |
| Fallback arrival | The property’s actual alternative when the service is unavailable. |

## Check the proposed page

Test one in-season stay and one winter stay. Can both guests determine which transport information applies, without assuming the state-park trolley serves their hotel?

**Evidence to obtain before adapting this to a business:** Current operator schedules, stop verification and any property participation. The tourism overview supports the distinction between services, not a claim about a particular hotel.

**Why this is locally grounded:** Three differently scoped local services make a generic transportation promise materially ambiguous.
