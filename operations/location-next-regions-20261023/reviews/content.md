## Overlap within the eight drafts

- **Allen and Hamilton:** Both use cabinetry, but Allen concerns evidence of what the business supplied or changed; Hamilton concerns what a paid design commitment delivers. Their artifacts and acceptance cases support different website decisions.
- **Monroe IN and Grand Traverse:** Monroe compares the output, area and planning horizon before selecting a design service. Grand Traverse explains turnover and responsibility after installation. The two tables are complementary.
- **Blount and Mecklenburg:** Both explain purchasing arrangements. Blount addresses a condition of a design engagement; Mecklenburg addresses the branding relationship on a commercial product. Neither needs an industry exclusivity claim.
- **Durham and Hamilton:** Durham explains the design work a prospect may need before approaching a builder; Hamilton explains design work a maker offers as a paid phase. The distinction is clear without claiming all firms follow either model.
- **Craven and Hamilton:** Both discuss stages, but Craven branches the meaning of an approval by project type. Hamilton explains the paid stage's output and commitment. Both expressly exclude approval/payment automation from the ordinary content brief.

The shared pattern of a focused improvement versus a fuller introduction is appropriate website-buying structure. Each draft supplies its own concrete information, questions and acceptance case rather than relying only on “public website versus operational software.”

## Optional wording improvements, exact fields

These are polish recommendations, **not integration blockers**:

1. **drafts/next-five.json → indiana/allen → decisionTitle**
   - Current: “Show the business’s contribution to the finished project.”
   - Suggested: **“Show how selected cabinetry and local customization fit together.”**
   - Reason: The current title closely resembles Alexandria's “Show the work you contributed to a finished display.” The body already supplies the useful cabinetry-specific distinction; the suggested title makes it visible sooner. No need to change the concept or remove either page.

2. **drafts/next-five.json → tennessee/blount → lead**
   - Current phrase: “put material purchasing conditions beside the offer”
   - Suggested phrase: **“put furniture-purchasing conditions beside the offer”** or **“explain furniture purchasing conditions beside the offer.”**
   - Reason: “Material” can be read as referring to construction materials instead of a significant condition. Naming furniture makes the opening immediately clear.

3. **drafts/next-regions.json → tennessee/hamilton → relatedGuides[0].label**
   - Current: “Understanding decisions in a project timeline”
   - Suggested: **“Planning a website redesign timeline”**
   - Keep path: /guides/website-redesign-timeline/
   - Reason: The destination concerns buying a website redesign. Explicitly naming it prevents a reader of the cabinetry process example from expecting a construction/design-production schedule guide.
