## Florence: useful purpose, link-control scope needs correction

[All Star Sports](https://allstarsports.net/webstores/) supports temporary shops and individual participant ordering, and lists the Florence address. It does not establish a directory, closed-shop page, URL retention or late-order policy. The draft correctly labels those as proposals.

Complete comparisons read: /areas/new-jersey/burlington-county/, /areas/kentucky/caldwell-county/, and /areas/virginia/colonial-heights-city/. Burlington already separates public service introductions from customer shops and names link maintenance. Caldwell already distinguishes group identity and shoppers from custom organizers. Colonial Heights covers campaign close, production and distribution. Florence adds useful handling of an old shared link after a short-lived store ends, with group/season identity and support preserved. It does not import Colonial Heights' production-after-close rule.

The unresolved issue is control of that old URL. A directory edit cannot repair an expired destination in an external store platform. The brief must state whether the business controls the shareable landing URL or whether the provider supports retaining its old page. This is an implementation scope issue, not an unsupported claim that All Star's current shops fail.

**F1 — required corrections (apply together):**

`decision`, before:

> If the store has ended, the useful next step is the retailer-approved contact route for that group, not an unrelated current store or an implied promise to accept a late order.

After:

> For a shareable landing-page URL the business controls, I would preserve the group and ordering period after the store closes and show the retailer-approved support route. An old link directly into an external store depends on that provider's closed-store options; changing the main website alone cannot preserve that destination. Neither route should imply that a late order will be accepted.

`scopes[0].supplies`, before:

> Approved group names, current store destinations, ordering-period wording and the contact route for an ended shop.

After:

> Approved group names, current store destinations, ordering-period wording, who controls each shared URL and the contact route for an ended shop.

`scopes[0].check`, before:

> A parent with an old link can identify its group and ask about that store without accidentally entering another team’s shop.

After:

> A parent opening a retained landing page on the business's website can identify its group and ask about that store without entering another team's shop. Old external-store links are checked against the provider's available options.

`planningTable.rows[2][1]`, before:

> Retain the group identity and explain its published status.

After:

> Retain group identity and status on a landing-page URL the business controls; check provider support for old external-store links.

`planningTable.rows[2][3]`, before:

> An old message does not silently send the parent to another group.

After:

> An old message linking to the retained landing page still identifies the original group and gives its approved support route.

This is sufficient to resolve the current Florence HOLD if the remainder of the record remains unchanged. Provider store configuration remains separately assessed under the existing scopeTrigger.

Reconsider only if new research supports a materially different, useful project decision; compare it again against both jewelry and multi-item intake pages.
