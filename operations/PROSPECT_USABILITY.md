# Five-prospect website check

Prepared for the strict audit follow-up. No real prospect sessions have been conducted or scored by this implementation. Browser QA is separate from this exercise.

Recruit five people who actually make website decisions for a service business, including people who usually use a phone. Ask for permission before observing or recording; use participant codes P1–P5 and retain only the notes needed for the review. Do not ask participants to send a real inquiry, enter personal details or pay. Use the local synthetic preview for any complete form task.

Give each task without pointing to a menu or suggesting an answer:

1. You need a small website and want to change its words yourself later. Find the suitable plan and explain the editing tradeoff.
2. Explain the first payment and what you would pay separately to other providers. Show where you found the answer.
3. Your existing service page needs clearer writing, and the site also needs routine checks. Choose between a one-time project and monthly help; explain what one month would include.
4. Find work that helps you judge the studio. Explain which examples are client work, owned studio work or fictional concepts, and what the evidence proves.
5. Ask a question about the plan you chose. Stop before sending on the public site, or complete the task with invented data in the local synthetic preview. Explain the selected service and what happens next.

For every task, record unassisted success, assisted success or failure, time, wrong turns, the participant's own words and the exact page. Ask what they expected after an error. Do not coach the answer, turn uncertain comments into success, or calculate a sales conversion rate from this sample.

Initial acceptance target: at least four of five participants complete each core task without guidance and correctly distinguish optional care, separate provider costs and concept work. Any false belief that a question takes payment, that a fictional business is a client, or that a plan includes unsupported editing is a finding even if the numerical target passes. Prioritize observed confusion, change the relevant page, and retest that task with fresh participants where possible.

Keep completed session notes in `.runtime/operations/`, with a review date and a defined cleanup date. Report counts with the sample size and limitations. Five sessions can identify usability problems; they cannot establish market demand or a reliable conversion rate.
